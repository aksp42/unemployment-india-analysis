05

Insights & policy notes

What the numbers above suggest for targeted intervention.

Shock, not trend

The spike was sudden and singular

Unemployment nearly tripled within one month of lockdown and began correcting by June 2020 — this looks like a shock rather than a structural shift, which argues for emergency income support over long-term labour policy change alone.

Geography

A handful of states carry the burden

Tripura, Haryana, Jharkhand and Bihar sit well above the national average even outside lockdown — recurring high-unemployment states are natural candidates for targeted employment schemes like MGNREGA expansion.

Urban vs rural

Urban areas swung harder

Urban unemployment peaked higher than rural during lockdown, likely reflecting the sharper halt to services, retail and manufacturing versus agriculture's continued operation as an essential sector.

Seasonality

April is structurally weaker

Even excluding 2020's anomaly, April shows a mild recurring dip in employment — worth checking against harvest and fiscal-year-end hiring cycles when planning seasonal employment guarantees.

Model behaviour

Employment count matters most

The tuned model leans most heavily on the absolute employed population and region identity, with the lockdown flag as a secondary signal — useful for early-warning dashboards that track employed counts, not just rates.

Limits

Read predictions with caution

With ~14 months of data and no unemployment insurance or informal-sector variables, this model explains rate variation reasonably well but shouldn't be used for granular, region-specific forecasting on its own.
